# Operational sensitivity methodology: Air India and Zomato–Blinkit

## Purpose

These outputs are decision-support sensitivities, not independent synergy forecasts and not management guidance. They make the missing diligence inputs explicit and show the scale of assumptions required to create a stated annual benefit.

## Zomato–Blinkit

### Delivery-backend unit-cost sensitivity

Eternal's FY2024-25 annual report reports 424 million Blinkit quick-commerce orders in FY25. The model applies a hypothetical saving per eligible order:

`Annual sensitivity (₹ crore) = 424m orders × ₹ saving/order × eligible order share × capture rate ÷ 10`

The low/base/high assumptions are:

| Scenario | Saving per eligible order | Eligible order share | Capture rate | Annual sensitivity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | ₹0.50 | 10% | 50% | ₹1.06cr |
| Base | ₹1.00 | 25% | 75% | ₹7.95cr |
| High | ₹2.00 | 50% | 90% | ₹38.16cr |

The saving/order, eligible share and capture rate are analyst-selected sensitivities. They are not measured savings. The public annual report does not disclose a matched-city delivery cost baseline, the proportion of orders benefiting from shared backend operations, or a counterfactual. This scenario excludes implementation cost, service-level impact, discounting, cannibalisation and dis-synergies.

### Cross-platform customer activation

The annual report reports 20.6 million average monthly food-delivery transacting customers in FY25. The model applies a net-eligible share to this gross audience, then assumes a conversion rate, incremental monthly orders and contribution per incremental order:

`Annual contribution (₹ crore) = 20.6m × net eligible share × conversion × extra orders/customer/month × contribution/order × 12 ÷ 10`

| Scenario | Net eligible share | Conversion | Extra orders / month | Contribution / order | Annual sensitivity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 25% | 1% | 0.25 | ₹10 | ₹0.15cr |
| Base | 50% | 3% | 0.50 | ₹20 | ₹3.71cr |
| High | 75% | 5% | 1.00 | ₹30 | ₹27.81cr |

The net eligible share is a haircut for existing Blinkit customers and customer overlap, not a measured cohort. The conversion and contribution assumptions are illustrative. Actual work requires deduplicated food/Blinkit cohorts, holdout or matched-control evidence, repeat frequency, contribution after incentives and cannibalisation. These sensitivities must not be summed with the delivery-backend sensitivity without checking for double counting.

### Interpretation

The FY25 order denominator is a reported operating fact; the monetary results are assumption-driven. Neither line is labelled as an independently estimated or realised synergy. The business's reported EBITDA improvement and scale growth are not attributed to the 2022 acquisition.

## Air India–Vistara

Air India reported that more than 4,000 vendor contracts had been consolidated at merger completion. It did not disclose the spend represented by those contracts or a rate-reduction bridge.

The model therefore solves the inverse procurement question:

`Required eligible vendor spend = annual savings hurdle ÷ (contract rate reduction × capture rate)`

It shows required spend for analyst hurdles of ₹100cr, ₹250cr and ₹500cr across 0.5%, 1% and 2% contract-rate reductions and 50%, 75% and 90% capture rates.

For example, a ₹100cr annual saving hurdle at a 1% rate reduction and 75% capture requires ₹13,333cr of eligible addressable spend. This is not evidence that Air India has that spend or can achieve that saving. It is a diligence test: procurement teams must provide vendor/category spend, comparable contract scope, unit rates, volume commitments, termination costs, transition costs and service-level outcomes.

The 4,000+ contract count is a reported integration milestone, not a spend denominator. No saving-per-contract average is inferred.

## Exclusions and next data requests

- No Air India fleet/network synergy is quantified without aircraft utilisation, route-level contribution, schedule and disruption data.
- No technology savings are inferred from the broader USD 200m digital transformation programme; it is not merger-specific.
- No synergy is recognised from contract or system consolidation without evidence of lower recurring cost and a net-of-integration-cost bridge.
- All scenarios exclude tax, working capital, capex and programme costs unless explicitly provided.

Primary sources: Air India merger completion release (12 November 2024); Eternal FY2024-25 Annual Report, especially company overview pages 3–6 and operational metrics pages 13–16 and 23.
