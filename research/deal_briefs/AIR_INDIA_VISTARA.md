# Air India–Vistara: outside-in synergy and integration brief

## Transaction context
The full-service airline merger completed on 12 November 2024. The official release says the combined Air India Group had 300 aircraft, 312 routes and 8,300 weekly flights at completion. It also documents more than 6,000 Vistara staff inducted into a new organisation structure, harmonisation of operating procedures across four airlines, alignment of 140+ IT systems, consolidation of 4,000+ vendor contracts, migration of 270,000 bookings and induction of 4.5 million Club Vistara accounts into Maharaja Club.

## Post-merger technology and commercial context
Air India's 2026 digital transformation article describes a USD 200m technology programme, modernisation of more than 140 IT/digital systems, a cloud-native technology estate and migration of 270,000+ reservations and 9.2m loyalty members. These are later company-reported transformation metrics; the USD 200m programme covers the broader technology rebuild and must not be treated as the cost of the Vistara merger or as synergy savings. The 9.2m loyalty-member figure is a later programme-wide population and is not directly comparable with the 4.5m Club Vistara accounts inducted at merger close.

Air India's April 2026 commercial transformation article reports direct passenger share rising from 15% in FY24 to 20% in FY26. This is a commercial transformation KPI, not a standalone measure of Vistara merger revenue synergy.

## Synergy hypotheses
| Lever | Mechanism | Measure | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Procurement | Consolidated contracts and scale purchasing across airlines | Spend by category, contract rate delta, volumes, implementation cost | 4,000+ contracts consolidated = observed milestone, not savings |
| Fleet/network | More coordinated capacity, routes, aircraft utilisation and schedule connectivity | Unit cost per available seat kilometre, load factor, aircraft utilisation, incremental contribution | Network scale observed; attribution needs controls |
| Corporate overhead | Rationalise duplicate functions and systems | Addressable overlapping cost pool and one-off restructuring cost | Hypothesis; no disclosed quantified saving in cited completion release |
| Revenue/network | Expanded itinerary choices, codeshare/interline connectivity and loyalty integration | Incremental passenger contribution, ancillary revenue, repeat purchase | Strategic potential; avoid equating network breadth with revenue synergies |
| Technology | Align reservation, loyalty, operations and back-office systems | Retired licences, run-cost savings, disruption and migration cost | 140+ systems aligned = milestone, not realised financial benefit |

## Integration risks
Airline mergers are operationally safety-critical. Risks include schedule/crew/aircraft disruption, fleet and maintenance complexity, labour integration, loyalty liability migration, customer experience consistency, regulator approvals and IT cutover failures. Synergy capture must not compromise safety, reliability or customer retention.

## Management vs estimate vs delivery
- Quantified integration outputs at merger close: 140+ IT systems aligned; 4,000+ vendor contracts consolidated; 270,000 bookings migrated; 4.5m loyalty accounts inducted.
- Later company-reported transformation context: USD 200m technology programme; 9.2m loyalty members migrated; direct passenger share of 20% in FY26.
- Quantified financial synergy target / realised savings: not stated in the cited sources.
- Independent estimate: no numeric value until procurement spend, category overlap, fleet economics and integration costs are sourced.
- Important distinction: contract consolidation is not proof of lower contract spend; validate rate, volume, scope and termination costs.

## Interview-ready takeaway
"The clearest public evidence is integration scale, not a disclosed rupee saving. I would diligence vendor-category spend, aircraft utilisation and loyalty/customer retention, then only recognise savings once contract rates and operating unit costs improve after integration costs."
