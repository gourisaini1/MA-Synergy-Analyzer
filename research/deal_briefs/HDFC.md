# HDFC Ltd–HDFC Bank: funding economics are the headline synergy, not SG&A overlap

## Transaction context
HDFC Ltd merged into HDFC Bank effective 1 July 2023. This is a bank/NBFC combination, so industrial SG&A-overlap benchmarks are not the right starting point. The principal upside mechanism is funding substitution: replace relatively expensive HDFC Ltd market borrowings with lower-cost bank deposits. The principal offsets are the bank's CRR/SLR reserve requirements and the priority-sector lending framework.

## Funding mix and outside-in model
HDFC Ltd's FY23 Q4 earnings call reported:
- Market borrowings (NCDs and commercial paper): 45% of borrowings.
- Deposits: ₹1,52,111 crore, or 27% of borrowings.
- Term loans, ECBs and NHB refinance: 28% of borrowings.
- Retail deposits: 73% of deposits, up from 62% in March 2022.

This implies total borrowings of approximately ₹5.63 lakh crore (₹1,52,111 crore / 27%) and a market-borrowing proxy of approximately ₹2.54 lakh crore (45% of implied borrowings). These are derived estimates from rounded management percentages, not audited exact balance-sheet totals.

The scenario model estimates annual funding benefit as:

**Market borrowings × substitutable share × addressable share × achievable share × funding spread advantage.**

Low/base/high assumptions are 10%/20%/30% substitutable, 70%/80%/90% addressable, 60%/70%/80% achievable and 25/50/75 bps funding advantage. These are analyst sensitivities; actual savings require matched-tenor funding costs, deposit balances, liquidity and refinancing data.

## Regulatory offsets
HDFC Bank's merger investor presentation states the bank's statutory CRR and SLR thresholds at 4% and 18%, respectively, and describes how these requirements would apply to HDFC Ltd's relevant liabilities. It also says Day-0 CRR was organically met and SLR assets generated positive net returns at the then-current yield curve; eligible infrastructure/affordable-housing bonds and existing liquidity assets could mitigate the effect.

The model therefore treats reserve drag as a **stress sensitivity**, not a realised cost: a 22% reserve share multiplied by a 40–80 bps opportunity-cost spread on migrated funding. The base case uses 61 bps. This must be replaced with actual NDTL treatment, statutory exemptions, CRR/SLR holdings, security yields and liability migration schedules.

Priority-sector lending (PSL) is recorded as an unquantified downside pending:
- eligible mortgage-loan classification and PSL treatment;
- incremental ANBC/PSL shortfall after the merger;
- actual PSLC purchase cost or incremental lending economics.

Do not assign a rupee cost before these data are available.

## Smaller operating-cost opportunity
HDFC Ltd FY23 standalone employee benefits (₹1,025.25cr), depreciation/amortisation (₹275.86cr) and other expenses (₹1,118.76cr) sum to a ₹2,419.87cr non-finance operating-cost proxy. The model applies only 1%/2%/3% duplication, then 50%/60%/70% addressability and 30%/40%/50% execution. It does not apply a cost-overlap rate to the merged bank's entire expense base.

## Scenario results
The model's illustrative recurring gross run-rate is the sum of:
- positive funding substitution benefit;
- small standalone corporate-cost overlap;
- negative CRR/SLR reserve drag.

PSL compliance remains unverified and is excluded from the net estimate. All inputs are low-confidence analyst sensitivities except the source-reported funding mix and regulatory thresholds.

## Financial trend and market reaction
HDFC Bank's reported cost-to-income ratio was 40.4% in FY23, 40.2% in FY24 and 40.5% in FY25; FY23 is pre-merger bank-only and not fully comparable with post-merger periods. FY25 NIM was 3.48%. NIM is an observed outcome, not itself a synergy estimate.

SEBI's adjudication order records that on the 4 April 2022 merger announcement, HDFC Bank rose 10.01% and HDFC Ltd rose 9.29% close-to-close. These raw returns include market and sector movements; the project separately estimates market-model abnormal returns using Nifty 50 as benchmark.

## Estimated vs actual: post-merger funding and balance-sheet evidence

| Metric | Model / pre-close expectation | Reported post-close observation | What it says (and does not say) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Funding substitution | Base-case gross annual funding benefit ~₹142cr, based on 20% migration of the market-borrowing proxy and a 50 bps spread sensitivity | Bank-wide cost of funds (including shareholders' funds) was 4.0% in Jun-23, 4.8% in Sep-23 and 4.9% in Dec-23, Mar-24 and Jun-24 | The bank-wide funding cost did not fall immediately; this is not a like-for-like matched-tenor test of migrated HDFC Ltd liabilities |
| Deposit franchise | Model assumes a portion of legacy market borrowings can be refinanced with deposits | CASA ratio moved from 42% in Jun-23 to 38% in Sep/Dec-23 and 36% in Jun-24 | Deposit mix weakened after merger; this is a headwind to low-cost funding, but not a standalone merger effect |
| Funding mix repair | Expected reduction in reliance on higher-cost borrowings over time | Annual report says high-cost borrowings were 14% at Mar-25 | Directionally consistent with funding-mix repositioning; the disclosure does not isolate the amount attributable to HDFC Ltd refinancing |
| Loan/deposit balance | No explicit loan-growth benefit is assumed in the synergy model | Credit-deposit ratio was about 110% at merger and 96% at Mar-25 | The bank consciously calibrated loan growth while deposits grew faster; this improves funding headroom but is not itself a synergy saving |
| Margin | Model's spread benefit is a 25/50/75 bps assumption on migrated balances, not a forecast of reported NIM | NIM on assets was 3.4% in Mar-24 and Dec-24, and 3.5% in Mar-25; FY25 reported NIM was 3.48% | NIM remained broadly stable; bank-wide NIM cannot validate the modelled spread on the migrated liability subset |

**Conclusion:** the actual record is mixed, not a clean confirmation of the ₹142cr funding-benefit estimate. The reduction in high-cost borrowings and credit-deposit ratio is directionally consistent with balance-sheet repair, while the increase in bank-wide cost of funds and decline in CASA after merger show why the model's assumed spread must be tested against liability-level matched-tenor data. These observations are not a causal estimate of merger synergy.

Sources: HDFC Bank Q1 FY25 investor presentation (HDFC-10), Q4 FY25 key parameters (HDFC-11), and FY25 annual report (HDFC-01).

## Interview-ready takeaway
"The value case is funding, not simply cost takeout: HDFC Ltd had a substantial market-borrowing book that could be refinanced through the bank's deposit franchise. I model that benefit against CRR/SLR reserve carry and keep PSL compliance as an explicit downside pending shortfall data. The small corporate-overlap estimate is deliberately secondary."
