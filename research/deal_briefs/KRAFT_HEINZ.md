# Kraft–Heinz: cost synergies were delivered, but value creation failed the wider test

## Transaction context
Kraft Foods Group and H.J. Heinz announced their merger on 25 March 2015 and closed on 2 July 2015. Management announced a USD 1.5bn annual cost-savings run-rate target by the end of 2017.

## Four-way synergy record
| Classification | Evidence | Value | Interpretation |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Announced | March 2015 SEC merger filing | USD 1.5bn annual run-rate | Management target by end-2017 |
| Estimated | Outside-in sensitivity in model | USD 0.71bn / 1.40bn / 2.04bn | Low/base/high gross annual run-rate; low confidence and explicitly ex-post calibrated |
| Realised | 2016 Q3 SEC 10-Q | USD 990m cumulative | Cumulative pre-tax savings through 2 Oct 2016 |
| Realised | 2018 post-integration presentation | More than USD 1.7bn cumulative | Reported cumulative savings by end-2017, net of approximately USD 200m business investments |
| Unverified | Sustainable net economic benefit | Not isolated | Requires recurring run-rate reconciliation, one-off cost, capex and revenue/dis-synergy analysis |

## Estimation honesty and hindsight
The USD 23bn cost pool is an analyst proxy, not an audited combined cost base. The 7%/9%/11% overlap range is **not anchored to a cited external benchmark**. It was selected as an ex-post sensitivity after the USD 1.5bn target and reported savings were already public. The resulting USD 1.40bn base case is therefore not a blind prediction and must not be presented as independent validation of management's target.

A true ex-ante estimate would require pre-close COGS, SG&A, procurement category spend, plant footprint, freight, headcount and vendor-rate data, plus a comparable-deal benchmark set.

## Integration cost and value destruction
The integration programme was multi-year, not a year-one cash outflow. The FY2016 Form 10-K reported programme expenses of USD 829m in 2015 and USD 887m in 2016, and an expected total pre-tax programme cost of USD 2.0bn. The FY2017 Form 10-K later reported USD 339m of programme expense in 2017 and cumulative pre-tax programme costs of USD 2.055bn by 30 December 2017; approximately 60% of cumulative costs were expected to be cash. The company separately reported cumulative integration capital expenditures of approximately USD 801m by end-2016 and USD 1.4bn by end-2017. These are cumulative capex figures, not annual amounts, and must not be added together. The model's USD 1.9bn cost input remains an analyst scenario, not the final reported cost. It spreads scenario costs over three programme years and calculates first-year net contribution and a five-year discounted NPV at a 10% analyst discount rate. The discount rate and ramp are scenario assumptions.

The later financial record materially changes the conclusion:
- FY2018 reported goodwill impairment: USD 7.008bn.
- FY2018 reported intangible-asset impairment: USD 8.928bn.
- FY2019 further goodwill impairment: USD 1.197bn.
- FY2019 further intangible-asset impairment: USD 702m.
- FY2019 net sales fell to USD 24.977bn from USD 26.268bn in FY2018.

The 2018 and 2019 impairment charges total USD 17.835bn across goodwill and intangible assets. These impairments are strong evidence that the carrying values and expected cash flows of parts of the business were written down. They do **not** by themselves prove the merger caused all the value destruction: category trends, brand investment, portfolio changes, leverage, competition and management execution also matter.

## Interview-ready conclusion
"Synergies were delivered, but value was destroyed anyway: Kraft Heinz reported more than USD 1.7bn cumulative savings, yet recorded USD 15.9bn of goodwill and intangible impairments in 2018 and another USD 1.9bn in 2019. That is why I distinguish synergy delivery from total shareholder value creation. The impairment is not a clean causal estimate of merger NPV, but it changes the diligence conclusion."
